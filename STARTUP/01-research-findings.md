### Growth Drivers

Construction grew 15.5% year-over-year in Q1 2026. Industry expanded 8% YoY in the same period. The economy has been one of the fastest growing in Central Asia since President Mirziyoyev initiated structural reforms in 2017, including currency liberalization, market opening, and privatization of state enterprises.

The country must create approximately 10 million additional jobs in the next 7-10 years to absorb its young, growing population and reduce dependence on labor migration. This demographic pressure is the single largest macro driver for startup opportunities: any business that creates employment at scale has structural tailwinds and likely government support.

Gold exports have been suspended, which impacts forex reserves and creates fiscal pressure to diversify export earnings into services and technology.

### Retail and Consumer Market

The retail market is approximately $17 billion and undergoing rapid modernization. Traditional bazaars are being supplemented and replaced by supermarket chains (Korzinka.uz, Makro) and international entrants (Carrefour). Online retail is nascent but growing quickly. Payment digitization is accelerating.

## 2. IT Sector & IT Park

### IT Park Overview

IT Park Uzbekistan was established on July 24, 2019, as the government's primary vehicle to grow the tech sector. It functions as a special economic zone, accelerator ecosystem, and policy advocacy body rolled into one.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Member companies | 2,800+ |
| Employees (end 2024) | 38,600 (146% increase from 2023) |
| IT exports geography | North America 46%, Europe/UK 25%, CIS 13%, APAC 11%, MENA 5% |
| Export breakdown | IT services 53%, BPO 28%, GameDev/Creative 11%, Software products 8% |

### Tax Incentives (Valid Until 2040)

| Tax/Duty | Rate for IT Park Members |
|----------|------------------------|
| Corporate income tax | 0% |
| Unified social payments | 0% |
| Personal income tax | 7.5% (vs. standard 12%) |
| Customs duties on imports for own needs | 0% |

These are not negotiated case-by-case. Any company registered with IT Park automatically receives them. Registration requires demonstrating that >80% of revenue comes from IT/tech services.

### Programs and Infrastructure

**IT Visa:** 3-year multiple-entry visa for foreign IT workers. Enables hiring international talent or attracting digital nomads.

**Zero Risk Program:** Designed to attract outsourcing companies. Provides free office space for 12 months, equipment for up to 100 employees, and wage reimbursements during ramp-up. Explicitly targets BPO/IT services companies looking to set up delivery centers.

**$5 Billion DataVolt Green Data Center:** Under construction. When completed, this will be Central Asia's largest data center, powered by renewable energy. Positions Uzbekistan as a potential regional hub for compute-intensive workloads including AI model training and inference.

**IT Park Ventures:** $10 million early-stage fund managed by IT Park. Investments made include Unitlab AI ($200K), Geomotive ($200K), and Datatruck ($250K). Check sizes are $150K-$300K for pre-seed/seed stage local startups.

**President Tech Award:** Annual competition with $1 million total prize fund. Signals government commitment and creates publicity for tech ventures.

**IT Park University:** Established in partnership with EPAM Systems to train software engineers. Curriculum aligned with industry needs, graduates pipeline directly into member companies.

### International Partnerships

IT Park has signed partnerships with Plug and Play (Silicon Valley accelerator), Alchemist Accelerator (enterprise SaaS focused), StartX (Stanford's startup accelerator), and MIT Legatum Center. These provide deal flow channels, mentorship access, and credibility for member companies seeking international customers or investors.

### International Offices

IT Park operates offices in: Delaware (USA), Heidelberg (Germany), Seoul (South Korea), Riyadh (Saudi Arabia), Tokyo (Japan), and Shanghai (China). These serve as business development outposts for Uzbek IT companies seeking export clients.

### Sector Goals

| Target Metric | Goal |
|--------------|------|
| IT exports | $5 billion annually |
| Registered companies | 10,000 |
| Jobs created | 300,000 |
| Infrastructure | 1,000,000+ sqm |

### Startup Ecosystem

The IT Park database tracks 1,500+ startups that have collectively raised over $300 million. The ecosystem is nascent by global standards but growing rapidly. Most startups focus on outsourcing or local digitization plays. Pure AI/ML companies are rare, which represents an opportunity gap.

## 3. Digital Uzbekistan 2030 & National AI Strategy

### National AI Development Strategy (2024-2030)

The government approved a dedicated National AI Development Strategy covering 2024-2030. This signals political commitment to AI as a priority sector. The strategy encompasses:

- Building domestic AI research capacity
- Deploying AI in government services
- Creating AI-focused education programs
- Establishing regulatory frameworks for AI use
- Attracting international AI companies to operate locally

### World Bank Digital Inclusion Project

The World Bank's "Uzbekistan Digital Inclusion Project" is actively funding:

- Expansion of digital infrastructure (broadband, last-mile connectivity)
- IT sector growth initiatives
- Digital literacy and education access programs
- Target: 6,200+ young professionals trained (50% women, 4% people with disabilities)

### E-Government Initiatives

The my.gov.uz portal centralizes government services online. Digital ID systems are being rolled out. These create both infrastructure (identity verification, payment rails) and demand (government procurement of tech solutions) for startups.

The government is actively digitizing processes across ministries, creating procurement opportunities for AI-powered document processing, analytics, and automation tools.

## 4. Workforce Analysis

### Labor Force Overview

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total labor force | 21.4 million (2025) |
| Youth unemployment | 11.4% |
| Migrant workers abroad | 3.5-4 million (25% of labor force) |
| Literacy rate | Near universal |
| Labor force by sector | Agriculture 25.9%, Industry 13.2%, Services 60.9% |
| Average wage global ranking | 117th |

### Key Workforce Dynamics

The 3.5-4 million migrant workers abroad (primarily in Russia and Kazakhstan) represent both a challenge and an opportunity. Remittances are a major GDP component, but these workers are also a potential talent pool if attractive domestic opportunities emerge. Companies offering competitive wages (even $800-1,500/month) can attract educated workers who would otherwise emigrate.

Youth unemployment at 11.4% combined with a young, growing population means there is abundant labor supply. The challenge is skills matching, not labor availability.

Average wages are low by Western standards, making Uzbekistan cost-competitive for outsourcing and AI annotation/labeling work. A senior software engineer earns $1,500-3,000/month locally; a junior developer $400-800/month. This is 3-5x cheaper than Eastern European hubs and 8-15x cheaper than US rates.

### University Ecosystem

| Institution | Focus |
|-------------|-------|
| Westminster International University Tashkent | Business, IT, Economics (UK curriculum) |
| Turin Polytechnic University in Tashkent | Engineering, Computer Science (Italian curriculum) |
| MDIS Tashkent | Business, IT (Singapore curriculum) |
| TEAM University | Technology, Engineering (local, practice-oriented) |
| IT Park University (with EPAM) | Software Engineering, QA, DevOps |
| Tashkent University of IT (TUIT) | CS, Telecom, Cybersecurity (largest local IT university) |
| Inha University in Tashkent | CS, Engineering (Korean curriculum) |

Multiple international university branches produce graduates trained on global curricula. IT Park University with EPAM specifically trains students for commercial software development. The pipeline is growing but still insufficient for the 300,000 IT jobs target.

### Talent Gaps

The existing talent pool is strongest in: web development (JavaScript/React, PHP/Laravel), mobile development (Flutter, React Native), manual QA, and basic data analysis.

Gaps exist in: machine learning engineering, MLOps, data engineering at scale, NLP (especially for Uzbek/Turkic languages), computer vision, and AI product management. These gaps mean that any AI startup must invest in training or hire internationally for senior technical roles, but can build junior teams locally at low cost.

## 5. Investment Landscape

### Multilateral Development Banks

| Institution | Role |
|-------------|------|
| EBRD | Leading funder in Central Asia for Uzbekistan. Finances infrastructure, private sector, and financial institutions. |
| World Bank | ~$1 billion loan package for strategic reforms (2022). Active in digital, energy, education. |
| Asian Development Bank (ADB) | Active in transport, energy, urban development, and increasingly digital infrastructure. |
| Eurasian Development Bank | Uzbekistan is the 7th member with 10% equity stake. Finances regional integration projects. |

### Venture Capital

The VC ecosystem in Uzbekistan is nascent. Key players:

**IT Park Ventures** - $10M fund, $150K-$300K checks, pre-seed/seed. The only institutional local VC specifically for tech startups.

**Sturgeon Capital** - London-based, Central Asia focused. Invests in growth-stage companies across the region. Has made investments in Uzbek companies.

**Regional/international funds** entering: Some Russian-origin funds relocated to Uzbekistan post-2022. A few Middle Eastern family offices exploring the market.

Most local startups still rely on bootstrapping, government grants (President Tech Award), or angel investment from successful diaspora entrepreneurs. Series A and beyond requires going to international investors (Dubai, London, Singapore).

### Investment Climate

Since 2017 liberalization, FDI has increased but remains low per capita compared to other CIS countries. The government actively courts foreign investment with tax incentives, free economic zones, and bilateral investment treaties. The currency (som) is now freely convertible, removing a major historical barrier.

Risk factors for investors include: political concentration (presidential system), underdeveloped capital markets, limited exit options domestically, and geopolitical proximity to Russia. Mitigating factors: rapid GDP growth, young demographics, government commitment to reform, and IT Park's tax regime.

## 6. Retail and Consumer Market

### Market Structure

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total retail market | ~$17 billion |
| Structure | Transitioning from traditional bazaars to modern retail |
| Key chains | Korzinka.uz (largest local), Makro, Carrefour (entering) |
| E-commerce | Nascent but growing rapidly |
| Payment methods | Cash still dominant, card/mobile payments growing fast |

### Digitization Opportunity

Most SMEs in retail operate with minimal technology: paper-based inventory, manual accounting, WhatsApp-based customer communication. Enterprise resource planning, point-of-sale systems, CRM, and supply chain management are all underpenetrated.

Food delivery apps (Yandex Eats via Yandex Go, local players) have established consumer comfort with digital ordering. Marketplace platforms (Uzum, OLX Uzbekistan) are growing. The infrastructure for digital commerce exists; the AI layer on top does not.

### Consumer Demographics

With 38.2 million people, median age under 28, and rapidly growing smartphone penetration (estimated 70%+), the consumer market is primed for digital services. The young population adopts new apps quickly. Russian-language tech products have a head start due to cultural familiarity, but Uzbek-language solutions have unmet demand.

## 7. Key Industries

### Automotive

UzAuto Motors (formerly GM Uzbekistan, originally Daewoo) is the dominant manufacturer. Produces Chevrolet-branded vehicles for domestic and regional markets. The automotive sector has a complete supply chain including parts manufacturers. Opportunity: AI-powered quality control, predictive maintenance, supply chain optimization.

### Textiles

Textile exports have doubled to approximately $3 billion in recent years. Uzbekistan is a major cotton producer (though forced/child labor issues have been largely resolved, leading to lifting of international boycotts). The sector employs hundreds of thousands. Vertical integration from raw cotton to finished garments is a government priority. Opportunity: AI-powered demand forecasting, quality inspection, production optimization.

### Mining and Resources

| Resource | Uzbekistan's Global Rank |
|----------|-------------------------|
| Gold production | 7th globally |
| Uranium production | 5th globally |
| Natural gas | Significant producer (primarily domestic consumption) |

Mining operations are large-scale and capital-intensive. Navoi Mining and Metallurgical Company (NMMC) and Almalyk Mining are state-owned giants. Opportunity: AI for geological exploration, predictive maintenance of equipment, safety monitoring, ore grade optimization.

### Agriculture

Uzbekistan is the world's #2 producer of carrots and apricots, and #7 in cucumbers. Agriculture employs 25.9% of the labor force and contributes 17.3% of GDP. The sector is transitioning from state-managed collective farming to private agriculture. Crops include cotton, wheat, fruits, vegetables, rice, and silk.

Opportunity: Precision agriculture (water is scarce in an arid climate), crop disease detection via computer vision, yield prediction, supply chain digitization from farm to market.

### Food Processing

Large domestic market plus export potential to CIS countries. Companies like Artel (home appliances, but indicative of manufacturing capability) have scaled exports from $5.6M (2017) to ~$100M (2021). Food processing follows a similar trajectory of modernization and export growth. Opportunity: Quality control, demand forecasting, production scheduling.

### Construction

Growing 15.5% YoY in Q1 2026. Massive infrastructure buildout (roads, housing, commercial real estate, industrial zones). Largely manual planning and project management. Opportunity: AI-powered project scheduling, cost estimation, safety monitoring via computer vision.

## 8. Competitive Landscape

### Existing IT/AI Companies in Uzbekistan

The local AI landscape is thin. Most IT companies are service/outsourcing shops, not product companies. Known players:

**Unitlab AI** - Received $200K from IT Park Ventures. Focus on AI/computer vision solutions. Early stage.

**Geomotive** - $200K from IT Park Ventures. Likely geospatial/logistics AI.

**Datatruck** - $250K from IT Park Ventures. Data infrastructure/analytics.

**EPAM Uzbekistan** - Major international IT services company with training center (IT Park University partnership). Not a startup but trains talent and sets quality bar.

**Various outsourcing firms** - Hundreds of small-to-medium software development shops serving international clients. Strong in web/mobile development, weak in AI/ML capabilities.

### What the Market is Missing

1. **Uzbek-language NLP:** No production-quality Uzbek language models for text analysis, chatbots, document processing, or speech recognition. The language uses both Latin and Cyrillic scripts, adding complexity.

2. **Vertical AI SaaS:** No local AI-powered vertical software for agriculture, manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, or education. International solutions are too expensive and not localized.

3. **AI infrastructure/MLOps:** No local providers of model training, deployment, or monitoring tools. Companies building AI must do everything from scratch.

4. **Computer vision for local industries:** Quality inspection in textiles, mining safety monitoring, agricultural crop analysis - all manual today.

5. **AI-powered business automation:** Document processing, accounting automation, HR analytics - these exist in English/Russian but not optimized for Uzbek business contexts.

### Regional Comparison

| Country | IT Sector Maturity | AI Ecosystem | Key Advantage |
|---------|-------------------|--------------|---------------|
| Kazakhstan | More mature, larger market | Slightly more developed | Higher GDP per capita, more VC activity |
| Uzbekistan | Growing fastest | Nascent | Largest population in Central Asia, strongest government incentives |

Uzbekistan's combination of largest population, fastest growth, strongest government support (IT Park incentives), and most aggressive reform agenda makes it the highest-potential market in Central Asia for a new AI venture. Kazakhstan is more mature but less dynamic and more expensive.

## 9. Regulatory Environment

### IT Park Registration Benefits (Full Detail)

To qualify, a company must:
- Be registered as a legal entity in Uzbekistan
- Derive >80% of revenue from IT/tech activities
- Apply through IT Park's online portal

Upon acceptance, the company receives ALL of the following until 2040:

| Benefit | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Corporate income tax | 0% (standard rate is 15%) |
| Unified social payments (employer payroll tax) | 0% (standard is 12%) |
| Personal income tax for employees | 7.5% (standard is 12%) |
| Customs duties | 0% on equipment/software imported for own use |
| VAT on IT services | Exempt |
| Property tax | Exempt for IT Park-owned buildings |
| Currency controls | Simplified procedures for forex operations |

### IT Visa Program

Foreign IT workers can obtain a 3-year multiple-entry visa. The process is streamlined through IT Park. This enables:
- Hiring senior AI engineers from Russia, India, or elsewhere without complex immigration procedures
- Attracting digital nomads and remote workers to spend time in Uzbekistan
- Building international teams with a local legal entity

### Subsidy and Grant Programs

**Zero Risk Program:** Free office space (12 months), equipment (up to 100 workstations), wage reimbursement during setup. Designed for companies establishing delivery centers.

**IT Park Ventures:** $10M fund investing $150K-$300K in early-stage startups. Application-based, focuses on scalable tech companies.

**President Tech Award:** $1M annual prize pool. National competition for tech innovation.

**World Bank/ADB grants:** Various programs fund digitization projects, especially those serving government or social impact goals.

### Data and Privacy Regulation

Uzbekistan's data protection framework is developing. A Personal Data Law exists but enforcement is evolving. For AI startups, this means:
- Fewer compliance barriers to building training datasets compared to EU (GDPR)
- Opportunity to establish best practices early and become the reference for local regulation
- Need to plan for eventual tightening as the country aligns with international standards

### Intellectual Property

IP laws exist and are improving. Uzbekistan is a member of WIPO and party to major IP treaties. Software is protectable under copyright. Patent system exists but is less commonly used by startups. For AI models and algorithms, trade secret protection is the practical approach.

## 10. Opportunity Synthesis

### Why Uzbekistan, Why Now

1. **Government alignment:** The National AI Strategy (2024-2030) plus IT Park incentives create a rare window where government policy, tax benefits, and funding all align for AI startups.

2. **Greenfield market:** Almost no AI product companies exist locally. The first movers in vertical AI will face minimal competition.

3. **Cost arbitrage:** Build AI products at $15-25/hour engineering costs (fully loaded), sell at $50-200/hour equivalent value to local enterprises or export at international rates.

4. **Talent pipeline:** Growing but still small, meaning early movers can lock in the best ML engineers before the market becomes competitive.

5. **Export potential:** IT Park's international offices and partnerships (Plug and Play, Alchemist, StartX) provide channels to international markets once products are proven locally.

6. **Demographic tailwind:** 38.2 million people, median age under 28, growing middle class, rapidly digitizing. The domestic market alone can support multiple AI companies.

### Critical Risks

1. **Political risk:** Single-party presidential system. Policy could change, though current trajectory has been consistent since 2017.

2. **Talent scarcity in AI/ML:** Must invest heavily in training or hire internationally for senior roles.

3. **Limited exit options:** No local stock exchange for tech. Exits likely through acquisition by regional players or international companies, or eventually through foreign listings.

4. **Currency risk:** The som has been stable recently but could depreciate given trade deficit and external debt levels.

5. **Geopolitical exposure:** Proximity to Russia and economic dependence on CIS trade creates secondary sanctions risk and supply chain vulnerability.

6. **Small domestic market for enterprise software:** Even at $181.5B GDP, the number of enterprises that can pay $10K+/year for software is limited. Export or high-volume SME pricing is likely necessary.

### Most Promising AI Verticals (Initial Assessment)

Based on sector size, growth rate, current digitization level, and alignment with government priorities:

1. **Agriculture AI** - 17.3% of GDP, 25.9% of workforce, water scarcity driving need for precision agriculture, government priority
2. **NLP/Language AI** - No Uzbek-language AI tools exist, 38M speakers, government digitization creates demand
3. **Manufacturing/Quality Control** - Textiles ($3B exports), automotive, food processing all need AI inspection
4. **Financial Services AI** - Banking sector modernizing, insurance underpenetrated, credit scoring for underbanked population
5. **Construction/Real Estate AI** - 15.5% growth, massive buildout, entirely manual processes
6. **Education AI** - Huge youth population, insufficient teachers, language barriers, government wants to train 300K IT workers
7. **Healthcare AI** - Underserved population, shortage of specialists, telemedicine growing
8. **Logistics/Supply Chain AI** - Landlocked country, complex trade routes, cross-border commerce growing
